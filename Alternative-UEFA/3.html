UEFA Europa League

The UEFA Europa League, previously known as the UEFA Cup, has stood as a cornerstone of European football since its inception in 1971. Organized by the Union of European Football Associations (UEFA), this annual football club competition serves as the second-tier tournament in European club football, positioned just below the prestigious UEFA Champions League and above the UEFA Europa Conference League. Born out of the reformation of the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup in 1971, the UEFA Europa League, often abbreviated as UEL, has evolved over the years to become a platform for eligible European football clubs to compete at an international level. Initially known as the UEFA Cup, it held the status of the third-tier competition until the discontinuation of the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup in 1999. The competition is still sometimes referred to as the "C3" in recognition of its historical place in European football. Participation in the UEFA Europa League is based on the performance of clubs in their respective national leagues and cup competitions. Over the years, the competition has seen changes in its format, with a significant milestone occurring in the 2004–05 season when a group stage was introduced before the knockout phase. Another pivotal moment came in 2009 when the tournament underwent a rebranding, resulting in its current name. This rebranding also included the incorporation of the UEFA Intertoto Cup, leading to an expanded group stage and adjustments in qualifying criteria. Notably, the winner of the UEFA Europa League earns the right to compete in the following season's UEFA Super Cup and gains entry to the group stage of the UEFA Champions League, starting from the 2014–15 season. Additionally, since 2023, the Europa League winner participates in the UEFA–CONMEBOL Club Challenge, a friendly cup against the winners of the CONMEBOL Copa Sudamericana. Spanish clubs have asserted their dominance in the UEFA Europa League, securing the highest number of victories with 14 titles. Teams from England and Italy follow closely with 9 wins each. The competition has witnessed the triumph of 29 different clubs, with 14 of them claiming the title more than once. Sevilla stands out as the most successful club in the Europa League, boasting an impressive record of seven titles. Colombian striker Radamel Falcao holds the individual record for the most goals scored in a single season of the tournament, showcasing the caliber of talent that graces this esteemed competition.
